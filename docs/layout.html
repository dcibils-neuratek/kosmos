Where things live

Kosmos has two trees. /kosmos is everything the system ships. /home is everything you have, and it travels: copy it to another Kosmos machine and you arrive with your applications, your games, your work and your preferences.

Agreed with Diego on 27 September 2026 (roadmap.md 6s), drawn before anything moves, and the documentation of the layout from then on. It is BeOS's hard line between what the system ships and what a person makes, with your own applications on your side of it. Nothing here is built yet; From today says what moves where.

/kosmos

What the system ships. The same on every Kosmos machine of a version, and read only while it runs.

  • apps/The applications that come with Kosmos: Tracker, Terminal, the IDE, Cafesa3D, Music, Camera, Processes, Monitor, Preferences, the Deskbar, the demosEach a window, -- kosmos: application; the Deskbar's menu sections come from each one's own header
  • programs/The console programs: ls, cat, ping, neofetch and 60 moreEach prints and reads lines, and is run by its name at a prompt
  • libraries/The Lua libraries: ui.lua, theme.lua, json.lua and the restuse("/kosmos/libraries/ui.lua") - today /lib
  • kits/The C kits a Lua program calls into: 3D, GL, PDF, compress, network, console, MP3, record, gameuse("/kosmos/kits/pdf") - today /kits
  • servers/The processes the system asks: console, ramfs, audio, network, devices, drives proposedRunning, not files you run; listed with their source to read
  • drivers/The processes that drive hardware: USB, network cards, the display, the power button proposedBy the kind of device, as user/drivers/ keeps them
  • kernelNebula, the microkernel, as the loader started it
  • fonts/ icons/ wallpapers/What the system draws with proposedIBM Plex, the Spleen faces, Haiku's icons - each with its licence beside it
  • documentation/The tutorials and the cheat sheet, opened in the browser proposed
  • settings/This machine's settings - its screen's scale, its network card, what its power button does, its sound card proposedThe machine's, not yours: a home carried from the ThinkPad does not bring its 14-inch scale to the M700
  • logs/What the system has said, keptToday log save writes /home/log.txt
/home

What you have. Yours to change, and it goes wherever you take it.

  • apps/Applications you installed or wrote, and the gamesEach in a folder of its own: its program, and whatever it came with
  • doom/Doom, with its WAD after the loaderIts engine is C, so it leaves the image once a program can be loaded from a file
  • quake/Quake, with id1 after the loader
  • super-nintendo/The Super Nintendo, with your ROMs after the loader
  • development/Your projects: what the IDE opens, and the tutorial's lessons to start from
  • preferences/Your choices: the look, the keyboard, the Deskbar, what starts at login, each application's ownToday dotfiles at the top of /home: .appearance, .keyboard, .terminal, .clock, .ide ...
  • documents/What you write and keep - and Cafesa3D's scenes and renders proposed
  • music/ photos/ videos/What Music, Photo and Video open first proposed
  • desktop/What is on the desktop proposedToday /home/Desktop
  • deskbar/The launchers in the Deskbar's menu proposedToday /home/Deskbar
  • trash/What you threw away, until you empty it proposed

Outside both

Three things belong to neither tree, because they are not files anybody keeps.

/dev

The devices: the screen, the keyboard, the audio ring, the processors, a stick's blocks.

/drives

Other drives by name - a USB stick, an SD card - as drives.html draws them.

/tmp

Memory, gone at the next start. Today /ramfs.

/app

The servers running now, by name, for whoever may ask them.

What a program sees

There is no global tree. A process sees what it was handed, and a path that was not handed to it does not exist for it - not forbidden, absent (layout.md §3). So the two trees are a convention every program can rely on, and each program gets the part of them it needs.

Terminal

You, at a prompt

  • /kosmos read
  • /home read and write
  • /dev the console
  • /drives, /tmp read and write

Doom

An installed game after the loader

  • /kosmos/libraries read
  • /home/apps/doom its own folder
  • /dev/screen, audio, keys to play

Not your photos or your documents: they were never handed to it, so it cannot name them.

Kosmos IDE

What you build with

  • /kosmos read, to learn from
  • /home/development read and write
  • /home/apps read and write
  • its own console for what it runs

Decided, and open

Decided - Diego, 27 September:

  • /home is everything a person has - installed applications, games, documents, photos - and travels to another Kosmos machine.
  • /kosmos is everything the system ships.
  • Doom, Quake and the Super Nintendo are home things, not bundled - which needs the ELF loader, agreed as 6t.
  • This drawing is part of Kosmos's documentation, kept true as the layout changes.
  • Changing the shipped system - your own window manager in place of ours - is set aside, undecided.

Open - for Diego:

  • The browser and Video. The browser is NetSurf and Video decodes with FFmpeg, both vendored as the games are. Home things too, once they can be installed?
  • A game's own files - Doom's WAD, your ROMs: in the game's folder as drawn, or in /home/documents or a /home/games of their own, so reinstalling a game never touches them?
  • The proposed names above: servers, drivers, fonts, documentation, settings, documents, music, and lower case for desktop and deskbar.
  • How a home is found at boot - today by its partition's GUID on the kernel's command line, so it belongs to one stick; to travel, found by what it is on any drive plugged in.
