Where things live

Kosmos has two trees. /Kosmos is everything the system ships. /Home is everything you have, and it travels: copy it to another Kosmos machine and you arrive with your applications, your games, your work and your preferences.

Agreed with Diego on 27 September 2026 (roadmap.md 6s), drawn before anything moves, and the documentation of the layout from then on. It is BeOS's hard line between what the system ships and what a person makes, with your own applications on your side of it. Nothing here is built yet; From today says what moves where.

The root, whole

Everything a program can be handed hangs from /, and there are seven names there, each a plain word that says what it is - no abbreviations, nothing left from before. Diego, 27 September: "systems should be self explanatory in their structure of files and directorues" - and, of the five beside the two trees, "yes, those five names are good, go ahead".

  • /KosmosWhat the system shipsApplications, programs, libraries, kits, servers, drivers, the kernel - and this machine's settings and logs
  • /HomeWhat you have, and take with youYour applications and games, each one folder; your work; your preferences
  • /DevicesThe hardware, as Kosmos presents itThe console, sound, the camera, the processors, a drive's blocks, the backlight - today /dev
  • /DrivesOther drives, by their namesA USB stick, an SD card, as drives.html draws them - today /drives
  • /NetworkThe networkAddresses, names and connections - today /net
  • /RunningWhat is running now, by name, for whoever may ask itThe window manager, and every window with what it lets you change - today /app, a name that reads as applications and is not them
  • /TemporaryMemory, gone at the next startToday /ramfs, named after the server rather than what it is for

And nothing else. /bin, /lib and /kits become parts of /Kosmos; /system and /user, two folders the disk is made with that almost nothing uses, go - what the system ships is /Kosmos and what a person installs is /Home/Apps. A program sees only the names it was handed, so a game may have three of these seven and a terminal all of them.

/Kosmos

What the system ships - the same on every Kosmos machine of a version - and, in Settings and Logs, what this machine keeps about itself.

  • Apps/The applications that come with Kosmos: Tracker, Terminal, the IDE, Cafesa3D, the browser, Video, Music, Camera, Processes, Monitor, Preferences, the Deskbar, the demosEach a window, -- kosmos: application; the Deskbar's menu sections come from each one's own header

  • Libraries/The Lua libraries: ui.lua, theme.lua, json.lua and the restuse("/Kosmos/Libraries/ui.lua") - today /lib
  • Kits/The C kits a Lua program calls into: 3D, GL, PDF, compress, network, console, MP3, record, game, FFmpeguse("/Kosmos/Kits/pdf") - today /kits
  • Servers/The processes the system asks: console, ramfs, audio, network, devices, drivesRunning, not files you run; listed with their source to read
  • Drivers/The processes that drive hardware: USB, network cards, the display, the power buttonBy the kind of device, as user/drivers/ keeps them
  • KernelNebula, the microkernel, as the loader started it
  • Themes/The looks the window manager draws inToday /system/themes - Diego, 27 September: "Themes in /Kosmos/Themes, yes"
  • Fonts/ Icons/ Wallpapers/What the system draws withIBM Plex, the Spleen faces, Haiku's icons - each with its licence beside it
  • Documentation/The tutorials and the cheat sheet, opened in the browser - and this page
  • Settings/This machine's settings - its screen's scale, its network card, what its power button does, its sound cardThe machine's, not yours: a home carried from the ThinkPad does not bring its 14-inch scale to the M700

/Home

What a program sees

There is no global tree. A process sees what it was handed, and a path that was not handed to it does not exist for it - not forbidden, absent (layout.md §3). So the two trees are a convention every program can rely on, and each program gets the part of them it needs.

Terminal

You, at a prompt

  • /Kosmos read
  • /Home read and write
  • /Devices the console, sound
  • /Drives, /Temporary read and write
  • /Network, /Running to ask them

Doom

An installed game after the loader

  • /Kosmos/Libraries read
  • /Home/Apps/Doom read and write: its program, its WAD, its saves
  • /Devices/Audio its sound
  • /Running/wm its window and its keys

Not your photos or your documents: they were never handed to it, so it cannot name them.

Kosmos IDE

What you build with

  • /Kosmos read, to learn from
  • /Home/Development read and write
  • /Home/Apps read and write
  • its own console for what it runs

Decided, and open

Decided - Diego, 27 September:

  • /Home is everything a person has - installed applications, games, documents, photos - and travels to another Kosmos machine.
  • /Kosmos is everything the system ships - the browser and Video among it.
  • Doom, Quake and the Super Nintendo are home things, not bundled - which needs the ELF loader, agreed as 6t.
  • An application is one folder, and everything of it is in it: its Lua, its ELF, what it plays, its settings and its saves. There is no /Home/Games - "WAD files are inherent parts of the game", and "I dont want settings and savedata from many programs to start living in other places".
  • An application's C is not a kit: Doom's engine is in doom.elf in Doom's folder, and there is no /Kosmos/Kits/Doom. Kits are what Kosmos ships for every program to use.
  • Applications are self-contained, on Kosmos's shared parts - its Kits, Servers and Drivers - and leave nothing anywhere else: "i want to be able to delete an app and all that the app brought,its gone". So an application keeps what it brings in its folder, and deleting the folder removes it entirely - a convention for now, not enforced: "we need to use the systme first before enforcing things that limit the usage".
  • An application's Lua reaches its own C as use("doom.elf"); its manifest is the -- kosmos: header of the program, the Lua file named after its folder; the applications Kosmos ships keep their settings in /Home/Preferences.
  • Every name begins with a capital: Kits, Libraries, Home, Deskbar.
  • Names are found whatever their case and keep the case they were given: /home finds /Home - "it doesnet make sense to have case sensitiveness in this day and age".
  • The structure explains itself: "i do want to focus on having an elegant and easy way to understand where things go and are placed in as sytem".
  • The root is seven names: /Kosmos, /Home, /Devices, /Drives, /Network, /Running, /Temporary - "yes, those five names are good, go ahead".
  • /Home is on the drive Kosmos started from, for now; on a drive of its own is for later.
  • This drawing is part of Kosmos's documentation, kept true as the layout changes.
  • Changing the shipped system - your own window manager in place of ours - is set aside, undecided.
