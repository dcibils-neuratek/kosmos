Kosmos Paint, a photo editor

The second of the Kosmos Studio family, beside Kosmos Cut. It has the same window, panes, inspector, toolbar and Export sheet, adapted to a canvas instead of a timeline. Simple develops one photograph, as Lightroom and Photos do. Full is Photoshop's ground: layers, masks, adjustment layers, selections, brushes, text and shapes. Both show the same document. Drawn on 22 November 2026, before any of it is built. docs/photoedit.md has what it is made of. The photographs and documents are made up. For Diego to change until it is right.

Full: layers, the canvas with a selection being drawn, and Curves in the inspector

The header is Cut's: the document's name and facts, the Simple / Full switch, then Open... and Export.... The toolbar is Cut's row of tools, here over the canvas. From the left: Move, Marquee, Lasso, Wand, Crop, Brush, Clone, Heal, Eraser, Gradient, Text, Shape, Hand and Zoom, each with its key. The current tool's options follow it, here the Lasso's: new, add, subtract or intersect, and feather. Then come the two colours. The layers take the left pane, where Cut has its library, because they are the document's structure. Each row has its visibility, a thumbnail, a mask beside it when it has one, its name and kind, and its blend mode and opacity. Indented rows are in a group. The arrow marks a layer clipped to the one below. The canvas is the photograph at 23%, drawn from the pyramid's level 2. The marching ants are a lasso being drawn round the lighthouse; the blue wash is the area it will select. The inspector shows the chosen layer, Curves: the histogram behind the curve, the channel, the points, then the layer's blend mode, opacity and mask. The foot shows the zoom and level, the tiles in memory against the budget, and the last step that undo would take back.

A selection, at 100%: the wand adding the sky to the lasso

Zoomed to 100% on the lighthouse's lamp. Shift with the Wand adds by colour: the sky at a tolerance of 24, contiguous, joined to the lasso drawn before. The selection is a mask in tiles, and the ants are its outline at this zoom, made in C. Select and Mask... (Tier 2) opens the refine-edge view for hair and soft edges. Feathering, growing and shrinking are on the Select menu, and a selection becomes a layer mask with one button.

Simple: developing one photograph

The same document in Simple. The left pane is Cut's library, here the folder's photographs. The inspector is Lightroom's Basic panel in Kosmos's look: the histogram, a Look with its strength (Cut's eight Looks, the same files), then light, colour, detail and effects. Crop and straighten is a button over the photograph, and spot heal is a click. All of this is one Develop layer at the bottom of Full's stack, so nothing done here is lost there, and a layer only Full has shows a "Full" badge here. A RAW file opens here first (Tier 2), with white balance from the camera.

Export

One sheet, shared with Cut (exportsheet.lua). It has a name and where, a format, a size, a quality, the metadata, and the facts the file will have. Location is left out by default. Several sizes at once and batch over a folder are Tier 2. While it runs, the editor and the desktop stay usable, and the "Exporting" window is Cut's.

One family: a photograph in Cut, a still in Paint

Cut's timeline with Harbour at dusk.paint on V1, drawn by the same Canvas Kit. When Paint saves, Cut sees the document's folder change and draws the new picture on the next frame. Open in Paint is on the clip's right click. The other way, Edit frame in Paint on Cut's viewer renders the frame at full size from the originals. It opens in Paint as a new document with Cut's grade kept as a Develop layer, rather than baked in.

Left out, on purpose

  • A catalogue of photographs, as Lightroom has. /Home/Photos is a folder and Tracker shows it; a Photos application is its own design.
  • Plug-ins. The filters are the kits', in C, and later on the GPU.
  • Learned tools, such as select subject and generative fill, are Tier 3. Their doors are drawn now, so they have a place to go.
