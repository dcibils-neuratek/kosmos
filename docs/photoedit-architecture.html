Kosmos Paint - what it stands on

The second of the Kosmos Studio family, beside Kosmos Cut. A photo editor is the thinnest layer: the document's tree in Lua, every pixel in C, in tiles. What both editors need is one copy, marked shared with Cut. What Paint brings is a kit wherever another application wants it, marked new. A brush stroke (B) and a curves slider (C) never put a pixel through Lua.

B - a brush stroke

  1. B1 A pointer event: Lua puts x, y, pressure and time into the stroke's ring. Nothing else.
  2. B2 The render thread smooths the samples into a curve and walks it at the brush's spacing.
  3. B3 Each dab into the layer's tiles; a tile undo still shares is copied first.
  4. B4 The damage composited at the view's level: below cache, the layer, the layers above.
  5. B5 The pyramid made again under the damage; level 0 behind, never waited for.
  6. B6 To sRGB, into the ring; the window lends it and paints only that rectangle.
  7. B7 The stroke ends: its tiles are one undo step. Nothing copied.

C - a curves slider, the stack recomposited

  1. C1 A slider's value is a struct field (params.lua); the stack is copied whole.
  2. C2 The curve's tables, 4096 entries a channel: microseconds.
  3. C3 At the view's level, 1.5 of 24 megapixels: below cache, the lookup, the layers above.
  4. C4 At rest, level 0 by the workers, visible tiles first, cancelled by the next move.
  5. C5 The newest view lent; one undo step when the button goes up.
  6. C6 The histogram from the same pixels.
