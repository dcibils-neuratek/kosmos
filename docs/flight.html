Flight

A small flight simulator of Kosmos's own: a light trainer over a real city you choose - its actual terrain under its actual satellite images - drawn by Vulkan on the GPU, with a graphics panel open to show what the 3D API does every frame.

"after the 3d api is done and running on gpu, i want to build a simple flight simulator app that shows the 3d api capabilities"; and the night it was drawn, "Can we use real world maps for the flight simulator?", "So i can choose a city to fly", "And see the actual satellite images" - Diego, 19 November 2026

The scene is rendered live by this page from the real tiles - Esri's satellite imagery over Mapzen's elevation, fetched as the Map Kit would - as a stand-in for what Vulkan will draw. Choose a city below; try the views and the time of day in the window's header, the switches in the graphics panel, and the pause button. Offline, it flies over an invented island instead.

What is in the window

The scene

The city's ground from elevation tiles, its satellite images laid over it, the sea, the sky and the sun, drawn by Vulkan on the M700's GPU through anv, and by lavapipe where there is no GPU. Haze fades the far hills into the sky.

Any city

Searched by name or airport in the pause sheet. The tiles come through the Map Kit - the kit Maps already fetches and caches with - so a city flown once is there offline, and Maps gains a satellite view from the same door.

Three views

Chase, behind and above the Kestrel; Cockpit, the six instruments under the windscreen; Tower, the airfield's camera following the aircraft. V steps through them.

The graphics panel

Why it exists: frames a second, GPU and processor time per frame, draw calls, triangles and terrain tiles, the device and how the frame reaches the screen - and switches that turn each effect off so its cost is seen. F3 hides it.

The instruments

Speed, altitude, heading, climb, throttle and flaps in a strip in the chase and tower views; in the cockpit, the six round instruments drawn as part of the 3D scene.

Time of day

Dawn, noon and dusk move the sun, and with it the sky's colours, the light on the slopes and the length of the shadows - the same scene lit three ways.

Pause

Where to start - on the runway, over the bay, on final approach - the weather and the wind, and whether the mouse is the yoke. Nothing else to configure.

Questions for Diego

  1. The imagery. Esri's World Imagery, sharp to about half a metre in cities, or Sentinel-2 cloudless, open data at 10 metres - a city's blocks, not its streets?
    Recommended: Esri's, as drawn here, with Sentinel-2 where Esri has nothing; the source is one line in the Map Kit, so it can change.
  2. Buildings. The satellite image is flat: towers are painted on the ground. OpenStreetMap has building outlines and many heights, which the Map Kit already reads for Maps; they could stand up as blocks with the photograph's roof on top.
    Recommended: yes, as the second step, once the flat city flies.
  3. The aircraft. One light trainer, gentle to fly, or a choice of two (a trainer and something fast)?
    Recommended: one, flown well; a second is a model file and a table of numbers later.
  4. Controls. Keyboard, the mouse as a yoke, and a game controller's sticks (F7).
    Agreed: all three, keyboard by default, a pad used when plugged in.
  5. Sound. The engine's note following the throttle, and the wind with speed, through the Synth Kit?
    Recommended: yes, it is what makes it feel flown.
  6. The graphics panel open on the first start, since showing the API is the point?
    Recommended: yes, open until it is closed, and remembered.
