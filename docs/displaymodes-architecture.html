Display modes - what they stand on

The page holds nothing: it asks through one Lua library, wm asks the window server, and the window server - the one process whose buffers must change with the screen - asks whoever drives the display: the M700's driver over its registers, or Nebula's HAL under QEMU. The monitor's bytes are decoded once, in C, by a kit of their own. A mode chosen (M) is on trial in the window server until it is kept, so a black screen undoes itself.

M - a mode chosen

  1. M1 The page paints the choice and posts display to wm.
  2. M2 wm asks the window server, WS_OP_MODE.
  3. M3 The window server asks the driver - or the HAL under QEMU - and holds wm's reply rather than waiting.
  4. M4 The driver sets the mode: under 50 ms of registers, AUX and a vertical blank. Then the monitor finds the signal: one to three seconds, its own.
  5. M5 Buffers of the new size, the kernel told the new screen.
  6. M6 wm fits the desktop, composes a whole frame, tells every window.
  7. M7 Fifteen seconds on trial: kept, or the mode before set the same way.
