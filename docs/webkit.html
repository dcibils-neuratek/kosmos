JetSki, on WebKit

JetSki, Kosmos's own browser, as current as Safari: WebKit underneath, drawn with Skia, each tab's page in a process of its own. The window is Kosmos's - its band, its menus, its keys - and the page is the web as Safari shows it. Drawn before it is built, 11 November 2026.

Diego: "I need a new web browser that is current and usable like safari or chrome", "We need to port either one to kosmos and have a proper browser".

Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest in the Solar System, after Jupiter. It is a gas giant with an average radius of about nine and a half times that of Earth, and only one eighth of its average density.

Its interior is most likely made of a core of iron, nickel and rock, surrounded by a deep layer of metallic hydrogen, an intermediate layer of liquid hydrogen and helium, and a gaseous outer layer. Its pale yellow hue comes from ammonia crystals in its upper atmosphere.

Its ring system is made mostly of ice particles, with a smaller amount of rocky debris and dust.

The tabs are the band, as Safari and Chrome have them: the menu button at the left holds File, Edit, View, History and Bookmarks (and under a desktop with a menu bar, Pewter, they are the screen's), the window's three lights at the right, and the band still drags the window. Below, back, forward and reload, the address field, a star for bookmarks, downloads (a thin bar while one runs) and the rest. Super with a number goes to a tab, Control+T opens one, Control+L is the address field. A link under the pointer shows its address at the bottom left. The page is drawn by WebKit's web process into a region and shown as it is - fonts, layout and colours are the site's.

Proposed, 15 November 2026 (Diego: "we need a progress bar in the bottom status section of jetski so the user knows when the webpage finished loading"). The status line carries the load: from the moment a link is followed a sliver of the bar and the host it is going to; then WebKit's estimate as a bar and a figure at the right; and when the page has finished, the bar full and green with "Done in 1.4 s", held for two seconds, after which the line goes back to the link under the pointer, or nothing. Stop or a failure ends it at once, saying which. The thin bar along the header's rule stays, or goes - Diego's choice.

Elements. The page's tree, the element under the pointer outlined on the page, and its styles with the file and line each came from. Sources, Timelines, Storage (cookies, local storage, IndexedDB), Graphics and Audit are Web Inspector's other panels, as Safari has them.

How it opens. Control-Alt-I, or Inspect Element on the page's right-click menu, which opens Elements on what was under the pointer; the ❖ button in the toolbar shows and hides it, and is there only while the developer features are on. In Settings, Advanced: Show developer features - on by default in a development build, off in a stable one. The inspector's own buttons, top left: close, dock to the right or to the bottom, and pick an element. It is per tab: each tab's inspector shows that tab's page, and follows it across navigations.
What it is made of. Web Inspector's front end is a web page of WebKit's - HTML, CSS and JavaScript, built with WebKit and shipped beside JetSki - and it runs in a page of its own, in a web process of its own, talking to the inspected page through the UI process. So the Kosmos port's part is small: a WebInspectorUIProxy for Kosmos that makes that second page, shows it in JetSki's window beside the inspected one and gives the split its divider, and the front end's files installed where it reads them. A page's crash, its address and the function it was in stay on the stopped page, which the inspector cannot outlive.
