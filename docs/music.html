Kosmos Music

A design, 14 September 2026, with Diego's four answers from the same day. Nothing here is built yet. The reference is VOX for the Mac. Basket Case is Diego's own file, on the disk now; every other track is an example.

What is playing on top, with what the file is and where you are in it; your music underneath, with the artist, album and cover read from each file; a dark look and a light one; and a mini player for when the music is all you want.

The window

VOX's shape, kept: the format across the top, the title large, a thin orange bar you can drag, the controls in one row, then where the music comes from and the list. The Library is every song on /home and on any drive plugged in, with its tags read straight from the file. Dark and light, both: View switches, and the choice is kept.

One engine, two apps

Playing a file is the same work whether it holds a song or a film: read it a window at a time, take it apart, decode it, keep time, let a person seek, and hand the sound to the audio server. So that work is one library, /lib/media.lua, and Music is its first face. A video app, later, is its second - there the window is the picture, where Music's window is a library - and it adds to the engine rather than copying it. BeOS split it the same way, with MediaPlayer for anything and SoundPlay for music.

What a video app adds, latertaking an MP4 apart, H.264 for its picture and AAC for its sound, and each frame drawn onto a shared surface when the sound's clock reaches it.

The clock is the sound's, and that one rule is what makes the second app possible: the audio server says how much it has played, the picture follows it, and nothing drifts. Decoding stays in C; what to decode next, and when, is Lua.

What it plays

Every format ends in the same place: sixteen-bit samples at the sound device's own rate, 44.1 kHz, which is all the audio server takes. sys.pcm already converts rate and channels on the way, in C, by interpolating between samples.

FLAC is left for later. When it comes, much of it is 24-bit, which sys.pcm does not take yet - written here so it is not found again.

Tags, read from the file

A small reader, beside the decoder, reads what a file says about itself: an MP3's ID3v2 tag at its start - title, artist, album, genre, year, track and the cover - or ID3v1's last 128 bytes when there is no ID3v2, and a WAV's LIST INFO chunk. The Library reads the start of each file as it builds the list, and keeps what it found while the app runs. Nothing is written onto your files.

What that gives up, said plainly: Tracker shows no artist column, and a playlist is a list you make rather than a search that fills itself. Both can come later from the same reader, by writing what it read as attributes, if they are wanted.

From the keyboard

  • Space play and pause
  • ← → back and forward five seconds
  • Super-← → previous and next song
  • Control-F search the list
  • Control-M the mini player
  • ↑ ↓ the volume

Left for later

FLAC, and the Deskbar replicant, at Diego's word. A video app, on the same engine. Radio is drawn and greyed: internet radio is an MP3 stream over HTTP, which the network stack can carry and nothing reads yet. Crossfade, the equaliser - on the roadmap already, in the mixer - and gapless playback come after the player has been used for a while.

Decided, 14 September

  2. No FLAC for now.Diego: "dont do flac for now". WAV and MP3; FLAC stays on the roadmap.
  3. Tags read from the file by a reader, and not written onto it.Diego: "cant we just gget the attributes from the mp3 file with a reader?" Yes - and that reader is needed either way, since attributes could only be written from what it reads.
  4. The Deskbar replicant after the player.Diego: "after".
  5. One engine that a video app can share.Diego, asked whether Music could play video too: "yes add the shared engine to the design". Two apps, one /lib/media.lua.
