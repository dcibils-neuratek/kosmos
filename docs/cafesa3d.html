Cafesa3D

A scene made of spheres, cubes, cylinders and meshes, placed and turned and sized in a 3D view, given materials and lights, and then rendered by ray tracing. After Blender, which Diego asked to study first, and smaller than it on purpose: one window, the things a scene is made of, and a renderer that does the light properly. Animation is the second version. Drawn before any of it was written (roadmap.md 4l), and approved as drawn: "3d tool is perfect".

The window

Blender's default layout, which the KitBash3D guide Diego sent starts from: the 3D view in the middle, the Outliner — every object in the scene — above Properties on the right, the tools down the left, and a foot that says which keys do what right now. It opens on a scene rather than an empty grid, as Blender opens on its cube. It works here: click an object in the view or the list, drag the view to turn it, Shift-drag to move it, scroll to come closer; Rendered ray-traces the view as you look, and the tabs on the right change what is selected — its place, its size, its material.

The scene is Blender's idea of a first scene grown a little: a ground, a red cube, a glass ball, a gold one, a blue cylinder, one light and a camera. The numbers in the foot are this scene's.

Adding things

KitBash3D's second step, and Blender's Shift+A: the Add menu, in the window's header and on the key. New things appear at the 3D cursor — the red and white ring, which a click with the cursor tool puts anywhere — so where a thing lands is decided before it is added. The Add menu in the window above works: add a cube or a sphere and it appears at the cursor, selected, in the view, the Outliner and the render.

What the sphere was made with stays editable, here, until it is edited as a mesh. Blender shows these once, in a panel that closes on the next thing done; keeping them is the one place this departs from it on purpose.

And a sphere renders as a true sphere — one equation, a perfect outline — while the view draws its 960 triangles. The same holds for cubes, planes and cylinders.

Rendering

KitBash3D's last step, Blender's F12: the scene through its camera, rendered in a window of its own, getting clearer the longer it runs. This one is rendering now, in this page, with the same method the app will use: every pixel a ray from the camera, bounced from surface to surface until it finds the light or the sky, thousands of times, and averaged. Glass bends what is behind it, the gold ball reflects the others, shadows are soft because the light has a size, and light bounces off the red cube onto the ground.

The times here are this browser's, in JavaScript. The app's are the ray-tracing kit's, in C with the vector units, and will be measured on the machine rather than promised.

Two ways to trace, one tracer. Preview is Whitted's 1980 ray tracing: a ray from the camera, a shadow ray to each light, and mirrors and glass followed exactly — no noise, hard shadows, nothing bounced; fast enough to turn the view in. Final is path tracing, as Blender's Cycles does: every bounce followed at random, so it starts noisy and settles, and gets soft shadows, light carried from surface to surface and glowing objects for nothing. Both share the scene, the materials and the hardest part — finding what a ray hits first — so the Render tab above switches between them, and the Rendered view follows.

From Blender's manual and the KitBash3D guide. Taken means as Blender does it, so that knowing one is knowing the other; smaller means the idea kept and the size cut; later means after version one.

What it is made of

PartIn Kosmos
The appLua, /bin/cafesa3d.lua: the window, the Outliner, Properties, selection, the tools, undo. It decides what is drawn and where; it computes no pixel.
The Solid viewDrawn by the GL kit (/kits/gl) as triangles with a depth buffer, which Doom's and the cube's frames already come through — not polygons sorted by distance, as this page does it.
The ray tracerA new kit, /kits/ray, in C: the scene as spheres, boxes, cylinders, planes and triangles; a bounding volume hierarchy so a ray tests a few of them rather than all; the two ways to trace; tiles handed to every core; the vector units testing four boxes or four triangles at once (roadmap.md 6l).
A renderInto a surface, a pass at a time, shown as it improves; Save as PNG writes it to /home/renders with the PNG writer the screenshot key needs as well.
A scene on diskglTF, the open format Blender exports: meshes, materials, lights, cameras and shared shapes, with what glTF has no word for — a true sphere, the 3D cursor, the render settings — in its extras. Read as data, never run. And OBJ to bring meshes in: the teapot, Blender's monkey.
