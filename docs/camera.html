Camera

The live picture from a USB camera, and nothing else. Drawn at the size the machine draws it — one pixel here is one pixel on the screen — before any of it is written (roadmap.md 6d). The camera is Diego's Logitech C920; Kosmos drives it over USB itself, as a USB Video Class device.

Live

The header names the window and, beside it, the camera and what it is sending. On the right the size — every size the camera offers, from its own descriptors — and ⋮ for the rest. The picture is at one pixel to one, black around it when the window is larger, scaled down and never stretched when it is smaller. Under it, how fast frames are really arriving: a camera that promised 30 and delivers 12 should say so.

What the window is made of

HeaderThe kit's ui.header: Camera, the camera's name and size beside it, the size as a dropdown, and ⋮
PictureA surface the app draws each frame into, converted from the camera's bytes in C — YUY2 by arithmetic, MJPEG by the JPEG decoder the video player uses
FramesIn a region the driver writes and the app reads, one ring, no copy through a message (CLAUDE.md: control by message, data by shared memory)
Foot30 tall: frames a second as they arrive, the format and its rate over USB, and Mirrored when it is
Window680 by 596 for 640 by 480 — the picture, the header, the foot and the frame
