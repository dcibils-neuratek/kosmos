Kosmos Cut, a video editor

One application with two depths. Simple is a story line with music, titles, transitions and a look, as iMovie has. Full adds tracks, the trimming tools, keyframes, colour with scopes, a mixer, multicam and proxies, as Premiere has. Both show the same project. Drawn on 22 November 2026, before any of it is built; docs/videoedit.md has what it is made of. The films and the project are made up. For Diego to change until it is right.

Diego, 22 November: "i want to build a fully equiped video editing app like adobe premiere or imovie", "lets work on a spec and a mockup for it".

Full: the library, the viewer, the inspector and the timeline

The header holds the project's name and the Simple / Full switch, which is the same project seen at two depths. Export is at the right. The library shows every clip as a thumbnail with its length. Hover skims it in the viewer, the blue band is the range chosen with I and O, and the green line marks a favourite. Bins are chips. The viewer shows the program at its own size, rendered at that size and not the film's, so every effect costs a quarter. It has timecode, J K L, a meter, and Proxies / Originals. The inspector shows what is selected, here the ferry clip: Video, Colour, Audio and Speed, and a diamond beside every value is its keyframe. The toolbar has the tools with their keys: Select, Track select, Ripple, Roll, Slip, Slide, Rate stretch and Razor. The timeline has tracks with their target (T), lock and mute. The story is on V1, a picture in picture on V2 and a title on V3. The films' own sound is on A1, Ana's voice on A2, the music on A3 (its volume line ducked under her voice) and effects on A4, muted. The cross dissolve is the light band, the yellow triangles are markers that become chapters, and the bar under the ruler is green for rendered, red for "will not play in real time" and grey for live. The yellow line is a snap.

A trim, as it is being made: the Roll tool on a cut

While a cut is dragged with Roll (R), the viewer shows the two frames either side of it, the out of the clip before and the in of the clip after, with how far it has moved. Ripple, Slip and Slide show the same pair. Slip shows the clip's own first and last frames, because those are what change.

Colour, with the scopes

The Colour tab: lift, gamma and gain wheels, then curves and a LUT from a .cube file. The scopes take the space under the viewer while Colour is open: waveform, RGB parade, vectorscope and histogram, each computed in C from the frame shown, at its size. A reference frame (here the harbour) can be matched to.

Simple: the story line

The same project in Simple. The tracks fold into a story line, and what is over or under it hangs from it. Removing a clip closes the gap. The squares between clips are transitions, and a click changes them. The inspector is reduced to what iMovie offers: a look with a strength, four sliders, crop or Ken Burns, speed and volume. Nothing done in Full is lost here: a clip with keyframes or an effect only Full has shows a small "Full" badge and keeps it.

Export

One sheet. A name and where, a size, a quality, and the facts it will have. Full adds the format, codec and bit rate, sound, chapters from markers and the queue. While it runs, the editor and the desktop stay usable, because export runs on threads below the window's. A notification says when it is done, and Cancel removes the partial file.

Left out, on purpose

  • Plug-ins. Every effect is the Edit Kit's, in C, and later on the GPU.
  • Themes and trailers. iMovie's templates are Tier 3.
  • Node compositing, as Resolve's Fusion. Layers and an effect stack go a long way first.
