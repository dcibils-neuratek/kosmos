Three modes as cards, each saying what it is for, what it buys and what it costs; the one in use marked. Balanced and Fair are the machine's policy, chosen here and applied when Kosmos restarts - a Restart now button comes with the choice (Diego: "perhaps changing policy needs a restart of the machine?", "i dont care if it changes it on real time"); Game mode turns on and off at once, since it is Balanced with a band and a core given to one application. Below, who ran: a lane for each processor over the last two seconds, coloured by band - the work spread across the cores, mostly normal, with short bites of display, sound and input. Beside it, the bands as a ladder, each with what lives in it, and a turn - the quantum - with what a shorter or longer one trades. Three numbers that matter: how often the processors switch, the longest the keys waited, and the slowest frame of a game playing.

Game mode Doom is in front

Doom has core 4 to itself.
It outranks builds and downloads; the screen, sound and keys still outrank it. Its frames are never cut short, and it wakes within a millisecond.
Frame times, a minute
game mode off, a build beside itgame mode on

Game mode on and Doom in front: core 4 is Doom's alone - one colour, a frame and a short rest, over and over - while the other seven share everything else. The ladder shows its band, below the screen, the sound and the keys. The frame times: under a build, game mode off, a frame every so often takes two or three times as long; on, they lie flat. The keys' longest wait does not move.

For Diego to decide

1. Which game gets it: the application in front when it is marked a game or chosen here (recommended), or any application in front?
2. A core of its own: move the normal threads off that core once, when game mode starts (recommended), or only keep new ones off it?
3. A millisecond timer while a game plays (recommended, measured first), or wait for the one-shot timer?
