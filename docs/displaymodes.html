Display modes

The resolution and the refresh rate chosen from what the monitor says it can show, on Preferences' Displays page: the monitor named from its own EDID, every mode it reports, which of them this machine can carry and why not the rest, and fifteen seconds to keep a new one before it goes back by itself.

The Displays page on the M700

The monitor first: its name and maker as its EDID says them, its size from the millimetres it reports, how it is connected and at what link, and the mode in use - the refresh measured beside the one asked for, the pipe's frames counted over the last second. Then every resolution it lists, largest first, with its shape and native on its own; and for the one selected, each refresh rate it is offered at, with the pixel clock each needs in small type. 144 Hz is greyed and the box under the rates says why, with a bar of what this machine's link and display engine carry and where the mode falls against it. The rendering modes stay below, unchanged (docs/rendering.html).

A mode just chosen

The screen changes at once - the monitor takes a second or two to find the signal, which is its own - and the bar counts fifteen seconds. Nothing answered, and 60 Hz comes back: the countdown is the window server's, so it runs out even if this window or the window manager has gone with the screen. Keep it remembers it for this monitor, by its maker, product and serial number, and it is set again at every boot; another monitor is left to the firmware. The same bar as the rendering modes', one component for both.

Under QEMU, with virtio-gpu

QEMU's own monitor, from the EDID QEMU makes: its sizes offered and set as on the M700, the window on the Mac following. The refresh rate is QEMU's word and nothing more, and the page says so. This is what the gate drives - the list, a change, the revert, the keep.

A screen the firmware drew

ramfb under QEMU, the T14 and the Mac Pro today: no list that cannot be acted on. The mode the firmware chose, that it cannot be changed while running, and the way to choose another at boot.
