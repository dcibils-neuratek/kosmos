It saves its own .present files and exports PDF and PowerPoint's PPTX.

What it does, first

  • A deck from a theme: Night or Paper, each its styles, its backgrounds and eight layouts - Title, Title & Subtitle, Title & Bullets, Section, Statement, Big Fact, Photo, Blank. A deck carries its layouts in its file.
  • The navigator: the slides down the side, numbered; a slide added after the chosen one with its layout, duplicated, deleted, dragged to a new place, or skipped.
  • A slide on the desk, fitted or at a zoom, its notes under it; things on it chosen, moved by dragging, sized by their eight handles, snapped to the slide's middle and edges and to each other.
  • Kosmos Write's six things, each the same thing it is in Write: text, pictures, shapes, tables, charts, comments - the tools' middle.
  • The Format panel: Slide (its layout, what it shows, its background), Text (Write's, and top, middle or bottom in the box), Arrange (place and size in points, front and back, aligned to the slide), Animate (the transition into the slide); and Document (16:9 or 4:3, the theme, the show).
  • Presenting: full screen from the chosen slide; on with Space or a press, back with the left arrow, a slide by its number, black with B, the end with Escape. The presenter's view - the slide, the next one, the notes, the time and the time since the start, and a bar green when the next slide is ready - swapped with the audience's by P, on one screen until Kosmos drives two.
  • Transitions: Dissolve, Push, Wipe, Move In, Reveal, Fade Through Colour, with a direction and a length, on a press or after a time. Builds, Magic Move and the ones that turn come later.
  • Files: .present, its own, a zip as .write is; PDF, a page a slide; PowerPoint's PPTX.

What is yours to decide

1. Write's pieces out into kits first

The caret and typing, the Format panel's parts, the pages on a desk and the tools row move out of writer.lua - and Office's package and charts out of docxwrite.lua - with Write moved onto them before Present has a line of its own.

Yes: the premise applied before a second copy rather than after, and Write's suites say whether anything changed.

2. What a slide is

A page of frames: each thing on a slide a small Write document set in its box. Text wraps at a box's width, and a long title runs past its box until shrinking to fit comes.

Yes, rather than a second layout engine beside Write's.

3. The slide's size

16:9 at 960 by 540 points, PowerPoint's widescreen, or Keynote's 1920 by 1080.

960 by 540 points, with 4:3 one choice away: the same slide, said in a PDF's and a PPTX's own unit.

4. What the first version holds

Transitions and builds together, or transitions first.

Transitions first, the six that copy and blend; builds, and the transitions that scale or turn, after the scaled blit is measured.

5. The presenter's view

On the same screen, swapped with the slide by a key, until Kosmos drives a second screen.

Yes, and the second screen written into the roadmap now, with Present as its first user.

6. PPTX in the first version or after it

After PDF, as Write's DOCX came after its PDF.
