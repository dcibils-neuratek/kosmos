What is wrong with it

  1. Lists three rows deep. There are three palettes today and the list shows all three; a fourth is a scroll bar. The font list has eleven faces and shows five.
  2. The fonts are the hardest part and get the least room - three lists side by side in 348 pixels, the family names cut (ibmplexsans-boldi), and no way to see what a face looks like before choosing it.
  3. You cannot see what is set. The role list says Widgets, not what Widgets currently is; finding out means clicking each role in turn and reading the status line.
  4. The order is the order things were added, not the order anyone uses them, and Palette default sits under Fonts while belonging to Palette.
  5. Two sentences pretending to be a picture. Window titles is a choice between two shapes, described in words.

What changed, and why

  1. Two columns, and it is shorter. The same seven groups in 668 × 530 instead of 380 × 580 - a window that fits a 1024-wide screen twice over, with lists six rows deep.
  2. Each role says what it is set to. Window titles — Plex Sans Condensed 15, all four visible at once, so the panel answers the question you came with before you touch anything.
  3. A miniature desktop under the family and size lists - a little window in the palette and faces being chosen, showing all five roles at once.
  4. Two lists for fonts, not three, because the role list above is the third and it earns its space by reporting.
  5. The shapes are drawn. Window titles is two small pictures of a window, one tabbed and one barred.
  6. Order of use, not order of arrival: palette, desktop colour, wallpaper down the left; type and window shape down the right. Palette default moves up beside the palette it resets, and says what it does.
  7. One status line, quieter, saying what is in force and whether it is saved.

What this keeps

Every control that exists today is still here, doing the same thing: the palette list, the sixteen desktop colours, a font family and size per role, the wallpaper list with none first, the title shape, the reset, and a line that says what was saved. Nothing is hidden behind a tab or a disclosure, because a preferences panel that hides half its settings is how the packing started.

The four questions, and what was decided

Asked on 20 September and answered the same afternoon - "1 two columns, 2 miniature desktop, 3 yes, 4 nothing". The drawing above is what those answers make, and it is what the code follows.

1. Two columns — agreed

668 × 470 rather than one column at roughly 420 × 700. Wider, shorter, and the lists get six rows.

2. A miniature desktop, not a line — agreed

The preview is a little window drawn in the palette and the faces being chosen: a title bar, a heading, two buttons, a list, a paragraph and a terminal line. Every role at once, which is the thing a single line of sample text cannot do - and the reason it is worth the day it costs. It is what BeOS's panel did.

It also answers a question the panel could not answer before: what a palette and a face look like together, without applying either.

3. Headings join the roles — agreed

Five now: window titles, widgets, headings, regular text, terminal. heading arrived with the style guide at Plex Sans Bold 18 and applications have been using it while the panel pretended it did not exist.

4. Nothing else — agreed

A light/dark switch, the pointer's size and the double-click speed were considered and left out: none of them exist to be set, so each is a thing to build first and lay out second.
