What you are seeing. The text is cut into tokens, pieces of words from a vocabulary of 51,200, which is all the model ever sees. Each token passes through 32 layers. In each layer, 32 attention heads look back at earlier tokens and pull in what they need, and then 10,240 feed-forward neurons transform the result. A head's cell is bright when it looks hard at one token and dim when its attention is spread out. The yellow bar under each layer is the share of its neurons that fired. The violet line along the bottom is the residual stream, the token's running meaning, which every layer adds to; it grows thicker as it gathers. At the end, the stream is compared with every token in the vocabulary, and the odds of each come out on the right. Xavier takes the most likely one, writes it, and the next token starts again from the left. Reading the question is the same passage for eight tokens at once.

What a person can do with it

  • watch a question become tokens, and see how many it cost;
  • watch each token of an answer pass through the 32 layers, and see which heads looked hard at something and how much of each layer's feed-forward fired;
  • click a head to see which earlier tokens it attended to, with how much of its attention, as arcs over the text, and read it in words ("layer 14, head 7 looks mostly at kernel");
  • see the candidates for the next token and their odds, which one was chosen, and how sure the model was;
  • see how much of each token's work went to the GPU and how much to the processor, and how long the token took;
  • pause on a token and step through the answer one at a time, back through the last 256 tokens recorded;
  • read a token's passage as text at the prompt, with llm inside.

Its busiest path, and its cost. Off by default: with no window open the kit is handed no record and does nothing more. Open, it costs one pass over values already in the cache per layer: a sum of 2,560 squares for the residual, a count over 10,240 neurons, and a maximum over each head's attention weights, which are computed anyway. That is a few hundred thousand additions against the 1.4 billion multiplications of a token, under a tenth of a percent. The odds are a softmax over the top ten logits, which are already sorted for the choice. The window reads the ring ten times a second and draws only the tokens that arrived. Nothing waits on it: if the window falls behind, the oldest records are written over, and the window says it skipped some.
