Four looks, one layout

You pick a look, not its parts. Each look is a finished design: its colours, its fonts and its Deskbar, made together. All four sit on the same fixed layout, so a window looks different in each and behaves the same in all of them.

The fixed layout all four share

These sizes are the kit's, not a look's, and they don't change when a look does. The fonts in every look are chosen so they fit inside them. That's what makes a look safe to switch: no window can move or break.

whatsizewhat fits in it
a list or menu row24 pxPlex Sans 16 (21 px tall), 8 px in from the left
a button28 pxPlex Sans 16, 16 px either side
a text field26 pxPlex Sans 16, 8 px in
a window's tab26 pxPlex Sans Condensed SemiBold 14
the terminal's line24 pxPlex Mono 14
between widgets, and from a window's edge12 px—
the Deskbar32 px24-pixel icons, averaged down from Haiku's 64s; Plex Sans 16

Text longer than its box is cut there, never spilling into the widget beside it: the third row above ends in "…" when the list is narrow.

The tab said 20 px here while the window manager drew 26; 26 is what it is. The Deskbar was 36, then a choice of 36, 44 or 52, and since 22 September it is 32 and not a setting.

Three more from 22 September, drawn above: a window's title bar is across the whole window again rather than a BeOS tab ("switch back the tabs from be os style to full width"); the Deskbar is each look's tab colour, its accent, rather than a colour of its own ("the deskbar tab color should be yellow or at least the same color of the acccent color of the theme"); and a scrollbar's thumb is that colour too, as Mac OS 9 drew it.

Size: everything at once, not the fonts alone

Diego, on the ThinkPad, where the fonts read small on a 14-inch 1920 × 1080 panel: a setting "like Windows does", "a factor multiplier of all the things in the UI", "Something like that slider of iOS". It replaces making the fonts two sizes bigger. A font that grows alone is what broke layouts before the layout was fixed. A size that grows everything together, the rows, buttons, tabs, the Deskbar and the fonts inside them, keeps every window in proportion at every step.

How it would be built, in one sentence per layer. Applications keep giving sizes and positions in the numbers they use today. The window manager, which already draws every kit window from its commands, multiplies them as it draws. It renders each font at its size times the step, so text is drawn at that size rather than magnified, and it averages icons down from Haiku's 64s. A window that draws its own pixels, like a game, a film or the cube, is told the step and chooses for itself. The pointer is divided back on the way in.

Decided, and one question

  1. The four looks, approved on 22 September: "Those 4 looks are great". Built as 0.10.109.
  2. Plex is a fresh machine's look, and the wallpaper is what Appearance keeps beside it. The Deskbar's height left on 22 September: "fixed at 32".
  3. The panel, approved on 22 September: "the panel is right, build it". Built as 0.10.111.
  4. The size slider, approved on 22 September: "the proposed size slider is great as it is", "with the %". Seven steps, as iOS has: 100, 110, 120, 135, 150, 175 and 200%, which includes the 1.2, 1.5 and 2 you named. At 150% the ThinkPad's screen is 1280 × 720 to a window, so a window wider than that has to be made to fit. Not built yet (roadmap 5z).
