Monitor, refreshed

The processor named whole, in the window rather than squeezed into its title; a graph a core with the core's number, its speed and its share; and each core's bar as an option in the dots' menu, off by default.

Diego, 11 October 2026: "Put the cpu name and model in the app not in the title as it can't be shown entirely", "each core bar is unnecessary", "Just leave the graphs with its history, cpu speed, kernel and user colors and core number", "Show the cpu name and model in a nice way", "Bars are a 'show cores bars' option".

As it opens: the processor, then a graph a core

The M700, rendering. The title bar says only Monitor and the legend; the processor has a card of its own at the top, its name as the firmware gives it with the trademarks taken off (Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-6700 CPU @ 3.40GHz becomes Intel beside Core i7-6700), how many processors and its rated clock under it, and on the right what the machine is running at now, across every core. Each core's graph keeps the minute it shows today, with its number and, above the graph, its speed and its share.

The dots' menu: the pace, and the bars

The three paces stay as they are. Under a rule, Show each core's bar, unticked to start with. It is the window's own choice, as the pace is: a Monitor opens without bars every time.

With the bars shown

The bars are today's rows, between the processor and the graphs, with the speed in a column of their own. The window grows to hold them and shrinks again when they are put away.

The T14's name, for comparison

11th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-1145G7 @ 2.60GHz reads as Intel · 11th Gen beside Core i5-1145G7. Under QEMU, whose processor gives a plain name, the name is shown as it comes. On an ARM board, with no brand string, the maker and the part are shown the same way they are now.

Sixteen cores

Still four across, now four rows deep, and each graph 60 tall rather than 84, so the window fits a 1280 by 720 screen. The rule, as roadmap.md 6h asked: the window is never taller than the screen. Up to 8 cores, four across at 84. From 9 to 16, four across at 60. From 17 to 32, eight across in a window twice as wide, at 60. Past 32, one graph for the whole machine, with a graph for each core one choice away in the dots' menu. With the bars shown at 16 cores, they sit in two columns of eight.

The processor's features

Diego, 13 October 2026: "can we add the CPU features to the CPU monitor app below the processors count and speed", "like VT-X, SSE, etc", and of the first drawing: "too big", "It's just one row of features", "It's a almost subtitle kind of thing".

One line under the count and the clock, set as they are - a subtitle: its virtualization and whether it is on, in green since Kosmos runs its sandboxes on it; its vector instructions, up to the widest it has; FMA; and AES-NI, SHA and RDRAND, which the Crypto Kit uses. Only what it has; nothing to open.

And the card condensed (Diego, the same day: "make the entire processor card smaller as it current too tall", "25% less tall and more condensed"): the maker beside the model on one line rather than above it, a smaller die, the model at 18 rather than 22, the speed on the right the same, and less padding - 83 points tall with its features line, from 116: 28% less, every line kept.

The T14 has AVX-512 and SHA, so its row says so. An ARM board says its own: EL2, Neon, SVE if it has it, and its crypto extensions. QEMU's processor, whatever QEMU gives it.

  • Nothing new in the kernel. It already hands Monitor CPUID's feature words, leaves 1 and 7, and the ID registers on ARM (sysinfo.cpu_raw), and whether virtualization is on (sysinfo.virt). Every name in the row is in those.
  • The names in one place, pulse.lua beside the processor's name - which word, which bit, what it is called - so About and neofetch can show the same row. Read once when the window opens: a processor's features do not change.

What it is made of

  • The name is /Devices/cpu's, as now (pulse.name()). Making it neat is a few lines in pulse.lua, beside name, so About and neofetch can use the same split rather than each writing their own.
  • The speed is new. Kosmos does not measure it yet. It comes from the processor-speed card queued after Quick Look: each core's running clock from APERF/MPERF, read by the kernel, which only it can do, and handed to Monitor beside the load it already reads. So this refresh and that measurement land together. Until a core reports a speed, the card shows the rated clock and the graphs show only the share.
  • The bars are the card Monitor draws today, kept behind the option rather than removed.
