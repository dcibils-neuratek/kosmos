What it does, first

  • Pages as they print: A4 or US Letter, upright or on its side, margins on four sides, a header and a footer with their distance from the edge, page numbers, facing pages, hyphenation and ligatures - the Document panel.
  • Text by style: Title, Subtitle, Heading 1 and 2, Body, Caption, Quote - change a style and every paragraph that wears it changes; on top of a style, a face, a weight, a size, bold, italic, underline, strike-through and a colour; four alignments, indents, line spacing, bullets and numbered lists, a drop cap - the Format panel, in its Style, Layout and More.
  • Things in the text: a picture with its caption, a table, a text box, a shape, a chart, a comment - the toolbar's middle.
  • The window: the pages on a dark desk; the toolbar across the top; Format and Document switching the panel at the right; View for the page thumbnails at the left; zoom.
  • Files: .write, its own; PDF and DOCX out, from Export.

What is yours to decide

1. The paper a new document starts on

A4 or US Letter.

A4, with Letter one choice away in the Document panel.

2. What the first version holds

All of the toolbar at once, or the core first: text and styles, pictures with captions, tables, the Document panel, the three files - then shapes, charts and comments.

The core first: it is a word processor before it is a layout program, and each of those three is a project of its own.

3. What a .write file is

One readable text file (the document as JSON, its pictures inside as text), or a zip holding the document and its pictures as files, as Pages' own is.

A zip: a picture stays a picture, a large document stays quick to open, and the Compression Kit already makes and reads them.

4. Opening Word's files

Export to DOCX only, as asked, or open DOCX too.

Export first; opening DOCX later, since reading Word's files well is the larger half.
