Automations

Tasks strung together and run on a trigger: open an application, press one of its buttons, send a mail, look at a web page, copy a file. Each automation is a trigger and a list of steps, chosen from menus and said in sentences. A background program runs them whether the window is open or not. What it is made of, and why every application has to become scriptable first, is in docs/automations.md.

Diego, 12 October 2026: "let's design an app called Automations, which is intended for a user like me to automate certain tasks in the OS. I'm basing my premise that every app is scriptable, meaning we can press a button, we can click an item, we can do whatever we want right now with the UI kit, right? From a script."

Every application the UI kit draws publishes its controls by what a person sees: a button by its label, a menu item by its path, a list's selected row, a field by its label. The picker shows the applications that are open, and each one's controls under its header, menu and lists. A press does exactly what a click does, through the same code, so an automation can do nothing a person could not. This is the larger half of the work, and it is the kit's: every application becomes scriptable without its author writing a line (automations.md, A1).

Let "Good morning" use Mail?

It will press Mail's Get Mail button every weekday at 8:00. Automations can then press Mail's buttons, choose its menu items and type into its fields for any automation, until you take it back in Preferences.

Don't AllowAllow

What you were not handed, you cannot reach. Reading what an application shows stays open, as reading a window's title is today. Pressing, choosing and typing need the application's name to be handed over, and the person hands it once, here. This is the consent step the roadmap already wants for launchers.
