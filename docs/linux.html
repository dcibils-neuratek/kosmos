Chromium fills the window - cage runs one application and nothing else. Linux's screen follows the window's size; the View menu also fits or shows actual pixels, and goes full screen. The keys and the pointer go to Linux while the window has them; Super and Kosmos's own shortcuts stay Kosmos's. Edit has Paste from Kosmos and Copy to Kosmos for text. The band says what it was given: Alpine, one core, 2 GB, the network outgoing. Downloads land in Kosmos's Downloads.

For Diego to decide

1. Chromium alone, or a Linux desktop. Chromium alone (cage) is the quickest to a browser and the lightest; a desktop (Xfce) gives a terminal and Linux's own applications. Recommended: Chromium alone first, a desktop as a choice in settings later.
2. Where Linux's disk lives. Alpine with Chromium is 600-800 MB, and 2-4 GB gives it room; the M700's /Home on the stick has 1.8 GB free. A bigger /Home (the stick made again with a 16 GB home - recommended), the M700's internal drive formatted as Kosmos's, or a disk that grows on the stick until the stick runs out.
3. How much of the machine. Recommended: 2 GB of memory and one core until Linux can use more.
