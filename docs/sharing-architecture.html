Speaks diskproto.h, so every program reads a share with no new client code; shareproto.h for what is not a file. One process for every share; a role of init.elf.

Keeps each folder's names and facts from QUERY_DIRECTORY, so a getattr never reaches the network; a handle per file being read; per server its dialect, signing, the NT hash while connected. The SMB Kit user/kits/smb/ · runtime/upstream/libsmb2NEW · N0, N2

libsmb2, vendored at 51c5910, LGPL 2.1: SMB 2.0.2 to 3.1.1, NTLMv2 in SPNEGO, HMAC-SHA256 and AES-CMAC signing, AES-128-CCM sealing, credits, zero-copy READ - and the server's coders, for smbd. About 25,700 lines kept of 34,566.

TCP to port 445: CONNECT answered at once, PUSH, POLL; RESOLVE. Two 16 KB rings a connection (tcpring.h) - the receive window is the ring, so at most a ring a round trip: about 30 MB/s at the M700's 0.55 ms

A ring sized by measurementELSEWHERE

C - copying a 1 GB film from the Mac's share into /Home, on the M700

C1Tracker starts cp --job and draws its bara process start C2read the next piece into the job's region19.0 us IPC on the M700 C3smbfs holds the reply; splits it into READs within the creditsmicroseconds of C C4each READ signed, its answer's signature checkedAES-CMAC a byte: not measured C5the stack: frames to the TCP ring, a window of 16 KBat most ~30 MB/s at 0.55 ms; never measured C6the Mac sends the dataa gigabit wire: ~112 MB/s C7libsmb2 copies ring to region - the one copy - and smbfs repliesno byte enters Lua C8the region written into /Home19.5 MB/s, the M700's stick

About 55 s, and the stick is why; the network's ring alone would allow 36. Writing a gigabyte into /Home needs a write in pieces first - today a copy stops at 1 MB. Playing the film from the share needs neither.

2,050 round trips here for four on the wire. The network is not where a listing's time goes; one getattr a name is, and /Home pays the same today. A LIST carrying each name's facts would fix both - measured at N3 before it is proposed.

A share is a disk

smbfs speaks diskproto.h, so Tracker, the Open window and every program read a share with no new client code. diskproto.h gains three errors and "as last heard".

/Network, two servers

The stack keeps every network operation; smbfs answers every file under it. One mount, two capabilities - no caller of the stack changes.

Nothing waits on a share

A question about names is answered within a quarter of a second, from the server or from what was last heard. Connecting is answered at once and asked about on the window's own clock.
