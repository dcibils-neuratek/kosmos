How the answer forms

A third page for the LLM Monitor, drawn before any of it is built. It follows one word of Xavier's answer from the text, up Qwen2.5's 24 layers, to the word chosen. Each layer shows which earlier words it looked back at, how much it recalled, and what the model would answer if it had to stop there. Press Play, or click any layer to see where it looked. The shapes are Qwen2.5 0.5B's; the values are examples.

What a person reads off it

  • The network is the tower. Every word of an answer is made by sending one list of 896 numbers up 24 layers, the same 24 every time. Nothing else happens. What the model knows is in those layers' weights, the 428 MB file.
  • Looking back is where the context comes in. A layer's 14 heads weigh the earlier words. The column says which one each layer weighed most, in words, and the arcs show all of them for the layer selected. "France" and "capital" lighting up in the middle layers is the model working out what the question is about.
  • Recalling is where the facts come in. The neurons of a layer turn what attention gathered into what follows from it. Research on models like this finds facts such as "France → Paris" mostly in the middle layers' neurons, and the page marks where the guess changes.
  • The guess column is the answer forming. Read as if each layer were the last, the bottom layers say nothing useful (" the", " a"), the middle ones a kind of answer (" a city", " located"), and " Paris" arrives around two thirds of the way up and firms. This is the part that answers "how does it output the words".
  • The resting place is hidden. Most heads put most of their attention on the first token of the text when there is nothing better to look at. It is a habit of the model, it carries no meaning, and it is why the Inside page's grid is bright everywhere. Here it is left out unless asked for.

For Diego to decide

  1. The guess at every layer costs as much as a word. It means scoring all 151,936 words 24 times, once per layer, which takes about as many multiplications as making the word itself. Done as one pass over the output matrix for all 24 layers, it reads the matrix once rather than 24 times. Even so, while this page is open Xavier would write at somewhere near half speed, about 9 words a second on the M700 instead of 18. That is to be measured. The choices:
    • a. Compute it only while the Story page is open, and say so on the page ("the answer is slower while you watch").
    • b. Compute it for a word you click, after the fact. The answer runs at full speed, and the kit keeps each layer's 896 numbers for the last 64 words, 5.5 MB, to compute from.
    • Chosen: b (Diego, 9 November: "go with b for the mockup").
  2. Where it sits. A third tab, Story, beside Overview and Inside (as drawn), or in place of the Inside grid. The grid shows what the heads do; this shows what they do it to. Chosen: a third tab, as drawn.
