Kosmos Cut - what it stands on

A video editor is the thinnest layer: the timeline's rules in Lua, everything per pixel and per sample in C. The Media Kit reads (and now writes) films, the Edit Kit renders a timeline at a moment - the same code for the viewer and for export - and the Playback Kit plays the mix in the audio band. A frame on the timeline (F) never passes through Lua, and neither does an exported one (X).

F - a frame, played or scrubbed

  1. F1 An edit: timeline.lua changes, and describe() hands the Edit Kit the whole timeline in fixed structs. Playing or a scrub says only a time; the newest wins.
  2. F2 The render thread finds the live clips and each one's source time.
  3. F3 Frames from the cache, decoded ahead by the Media Kit's threads; a miss seeks to the key frame and decodes forward - proxies make that one frame.
  4. F4 Each layer to 4:2:0-to-RGB and the viewer's size in one SIMD pass; grade, LUT, key, blend, the dissolve, the title.
  5. F5 Into the ring of three; a pulse.
  6. F6 The window lends the newest due frame to the viewer; only the playhead's columns repaint.

A - the sound

  1. The Edit Kit mixes every track - gain, pan, fades, EQ - ahead, into the Playback Kit's ring. Its thread in the audio band writes periods to the audio server. Its frames heard are the clock F follows.

X - export

  1. X1 The sheet starts it; progress is a counter read twice a second.
  2. X2 The same render, full size, from the originals, never dropping a frame.
  3. X3 RGB to 4:2:0, SIMD.
  4. X4 x264 on four threads, AAC; the mov muxer.
  5. X5 4 MB pieces through a ring to /Home, never the whole film in memory.
