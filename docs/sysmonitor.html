System Monitor

Every monitor Kosmos has in one window: the whole machine at a glance on the Overview, and a page for each part - its device's name, its clocks, its power, its temperature and its history. Process Viewer, GPU Monitor, Disk Monitor and Network Monitor become pages of it. Monitor becomes its CPU page. Every row they show today keeps a place, and docs/sysmonitor.md counts them all.

The Overview, on the M700

The sidebar always shows, one row for each part of this machine, each with a live number. Disks has a row for each drive, and the Heat and power row shows the package's temperature. The Overview is a tile for each part: its device's name, the one or two numbers that matter now, and the history window's graph - a small graph for each core, memory, the GPU's busy, each drive's and the network's in and out mirrored. A part the machine does not have gets no tile: the M700 has no battery, and under QEMU there is no GPU tile. The busiest programs are Processes' top five. A click on any tile opens its page. The header holds the history window for every page at once (1 minute, 10 minutes, 1 hour). The dots hold the pace and bytes or bits, as every monitor's do today.

The CPU page: Monitor, with its temperature, its power and who ran

Monitor's window, whole: the chip with its maker's mark, the model, its features (virtualization green), the clock now, and a graph for each core with the kernel's time in red under the thread's own in green, each core's speed and share above it. New beside each core: its temperature, which Nebula already reads on every core's tick. New under them: the package's temperature against TjMax, and its power against PL1 and PL2, over the history window. Monitor computes TjMax, PL1 and PL2 and has never drawn them. And who ran: the Scheduler's lanes and its three numbers, read-only. The Scheduler stays the place to change them.

The Memory page: used, the pools, who holds the most

Drawn after the code, for a look before it is folded (user/lib/sysmon/memory.lua, S6). Used over the history window, Nebula's part at the floor in a darker blue and the rest spoken for over it; at 64 MB of 7.9 GB Nebula's part is under one per cent of the graph's height. The four pools, each of the most it can grow to, the address spaces drawn for the first time (Process Viewer counted them and drew no bar). Who holds the most: the ten largest processes and Nebula, each with a bar against the largest. Elsewhere: the GPU's buffers against the GPU page's 256 MB, what /Temporary holds, which no server reports, so it says "not measured on this machine", and each drive's cache.

The GPU page: each part, its clock, its name, its power

GPU Monitor's window, whole, while Xavier answers on the GPU over ten minutes: the card with the device's name and id, its range and its clock now, a chip for each engine with its share, busy, the clock and its sleep, memory, the display, who holds its memory, and down the right the facts. New: the power in watts as a graph, not only as the number now, and the temperature. An Intel GPU on the die has no sensor of its own, so the package's stands in, and the page says it is the package's. Over ten minutes a column is ten seconds: its bar is the mean, and the tick above it is the highest second, so a short spike still shows.

The Heat and power page: every temperature and every watt

Drawn after the code (user/lib/sysmon/heat.lua, S6), over a minute in which a build held the package at its power limit for four seconds. The package's two graphs, the same pair the CPU page draws: its temperature against TjMax, and its power against PL2 with a faint line at PL1 and a mark under it for each column it was held. Each core's temperature, a cell a core. The GPU's power over the window, where there is a GPU. Every reading: TjMax, PL1, its window and PL2 in words for the first time, and the six readings no machine gives today listed by name, each "not measured on this machine" and never a nought. The M700 has no battery, so that row says "none on this machine". Under QEMU every reading on this page says not measured.
